The simple tax system for the 21st century.

Fair, social and innovative.

All taxes and levies are spread fairly across labour, capital and automation – at most one third on any income. Work pays, and the strong social net and the transformation dividend secure social peace. Municipalities decide more independently, and a European roof protects common interests by majority vote.

The concept:

Few rules, one principle

Every building block has already been tried somewhere in the world. The Golden Third joins them into a system that can be explained on a single page.

Income

Graduated rates with a hard ceiling: the Golden Third

0%up to €10,720
11%up to €16,080
19%up to €21,440
25%up to €32,160
33%above — upper limit

Gross income is taxed – no deductions, no income splitting. Basic needs stay tax-free; above that, work pays at every step. The brackets lie at one, one and a half, two and three times basic needs and grow with them.

Work

No more social contributions

0% on wages

Work is no longer taxed twice. Employers pass at least 80% of the employer contributions they save into wages. A law requires all employment contracts to be adjusted accordingly by the date of introduction. Denmark already levies hardly any social contributions today.

Capital

Capital and work treated alike

33% on all returns

Capital gains count too. Turning wages into capital income no longer pays.

Inheritance

Inheritance is income too

33% income tax on inheritances above €100,000

No exemptions. Whoever inherits a business or a house can defer the tax – nobody has to sell to pay it.

Land · municipality

The municipality taxes the land

0.1–1% of land value

Only the value of the plot counts, not the building. Whoever builds or renovates no longer pays for it. It may not be passed on to tenants.

Wealth

Large fortunes contribute

1.5% above €2 million

Wealth up to €2 million is untouched. Above it, a minimum tax applies that can be credited against income tax.

Corporations and platforms

Taxed where the customers are

33% where the customers are

This explicitly includes digital platforms. Profits can be shifted, customers cannot.

Consumption

VAT becomes a label

A+ · 5 %basic needs, repairs
A · 11 %sustainable goods
B · 17 %standard goods
C · 25 %resource-intensive, luxury
S · 33 %harmful: tobacco, alcohol, fuels, combustion cars

As with the energy label, you see at a glance what a product costs and why. Goods are rated by their resource and carbon footprint, not by origin. 33% applies only to harmful goods.

Climate

A carbon price returned to everyone

European price path · returned through the transformation dividend

Those who consume little gain. Switzerland has been returning its CO₂ levy to everyone in equal shares for years.

Dividend

Transformation dividend: every month, for everyone

Adults in full, children 80%, without applying. Above all, it returns what the transformation brings in.

The Strong Net

Nobody falls through

basic cover without premiums

Knotted from many knots – everyone holds, nobody falls through: health, care, a minimum pension, basic needs in case of disability and unemployment. Anyone who earns little is topped up to basic needs; every euro you earn yourself counts only by half. Beyond that is your own route with your own provision (10% of wages into your own pension account). Basic livelihood (Germany): €1,029 a month for a single person, €2,675 for a couple with two children; the net as a whole costs 9.6% of GDP (2028, today's purchasing power).

Strong municipalities, strong citizens, a strong European roof

The number of administrative levels is reduced. The municipality receives its share directly from the roof, decides on its own finances and delegates tasks to higher-level administrative associations (e.g. infrastructure partnerships). The European roof takes on only what no municipality can do alone – and may not take more than the treaty allows.

Why a transformation dividend?

What we produce today rests on what generations before us built: knowledge, technology, infrastructure. More and more of it is done by machines and software. The dividend gives everyone the same share back – above all what the transformation brings in: the carbon price and the special rate on harmful goods. Its level follows from each country's economic output and tasks. It shrinks as combustion engines and emissions disappear – that is intended. What carries the load is the strong net.

And it offsets what the transformation of work, energy and the economy asks of each of us: whoever has to change course does not stand empty-handed.

The individual is trusted

to manage their own money: at most one third in tax, no contributions, their own provision in their own account. A dividend every month, without applying.

The municipality is trusted

to run its own affairs: it receives fixed amounts per resident and per child, under the same rule in every country. It pays out top-ups and hardship support locally – the level and the crediting rule are set by the country. It decides on membership in districts and associations and on local support programmes.

The roof is trusted

what can only be done together: defence and digital sovereignty, a single market without borders, common standards. Nothing more. The roof decides by a majority of the delegates of the municipal associations.

Your municipality does its own sums

Distribution by the roof comes down to two numbers: €3,122 per inhabitant and €10,954 per child per year (average of the 30 states; every country applies the same rule to its economic output). More children therefore means more money for schools. Whatever the municipality actively saves on administration ends up with its citizens.

On average, 15.7% of a municipal budget goes to administration. Municipal tasks differ from country to country, so a direct comparison of shares only goes so far.

A net that holds – and opens paths

The Golden Third combines security with freedom: nobody falls below basic needs, and whoever starts something new – a job, training, a business – does not lose their footing.

Education and care

The municipality invests in the next generation

For every child aged 5 to 19 the municipality receives €10,954 each year – more children, more money. It funds schools, childcare and support locally. Children receive care, upbringing and education as services of the municipality; the child dividend and the basic livelihood cover material needs.

Your own education and work

Every step pays

No social contributions on wages, at most one third tax on every euro. Anyone who earns little is topped up to basic needs; only half of your own earnings is counted. The dividend continues – during further training too, or when changing jobs.

Research and innovation

Research together, found freely

Basic research and universities are among the common tasks of the roof (0.77 and 0.74% of GDP in the overall calculation). Labour, capital and automation are treated alike: whoever develops, hires or founds pays at most one third on every additional euro.

Culture and community

What matters locally is decided locally

Culture, sport and community stay with the municipalities (1.22% of GDP in the overall calculation). They decide for themselves what to spend their money on – including what they save in administration.

More security and more freedom at once

The net holds without holding you back. The dividend comes without applying and does not stop when someone takes up work. The top-up to basic needs falls by only half of what you earn yourself. High rents, special needs and emergencies are handled locally by the municipality – under the country's rules, in one place.

Fairer distribution? The Gini coefficient

The Gini coefficient measures the inequality of disposable incomes (0 = everyone equal, 100 = one person has everything). According to the model it falls clearly in Germany, Italy, Spain and Poland – dividend and basic livelihood lift the lower incomes. In France it rises in this calculation. Main reason: French employer contributions for health and family insurance have no ceiling; passing them into wages (80%) lifts high wages strongly, while at the minimum wage hardly any contributions exist and the prime d'activité disappears. If the pass-through is capped, the Gini falls in France too (27.4 to 29.6); this has not been decided.

The transformation dividend over time

Above all, the dividend returns what was paid in taxes on harmful products and behaviour. As harmful emissions disappear, this part shrinks; health and care cost more as the population ages. Growth works the other way. On average across the 30 states the dividend falls from €254 (2028) to €144 (2060). More work and automation raise it again.

Why the roof provides stability

A common roof is only as good as its limits. Five rules are written into the treaty.

The ceiling

The roof keeps at most 32% of what it collects for the municipalities – and only what its tasks really cost.

What is not needed flows through

Every euro above need goes to the municipalities, into the strong net and the transformation dividend.

Formula instead of bargaining

Municipalities receive fixed amounts per resident and per child. Nobody has to ask, nobody can cut.

Legacy burdens: a matter for each state

Each state settles its own old debts and pension promises. The roof offers tools: a common fund with a common interest rate, repayment by 2098 at the latest – and an orderly restart procedure for over-indebted states. Existing pensions continue, reduced by 7.5% – less than the health and long-term care contributions pensioners pay today, which disappear (12.35% in Germany). Small pensions are topped up to the basic livelihood.

Deciding by majority

An alliance in which individual members can block decisions with a veto cannot decide for everyone when the moment of decision comes.

What already works elsewhere

Many building blocks already exist somewhere today – often for decades.

Estonia

One rate, filed online in minutes

tax return online in minutes · land tax since 1993

The tax return is done online in a few minutes. Property tax is based on land value alone; buildings are not taxed.

Denmark

Strong municipalities, a welfare state funded by taxes

98 municipalities since 2007

Municipalities run schools, care and social services. Social contributions play hardly any role – the welfare state is paid for by taxes.

Switzerland

A CO₂ levy returned to everyone

since 2008 · the same amount for everyone

Revenue from the CO₂ levy is returned to all residents in equal shares, credited against health insurance premiums. Municipalities set their own taxes.

Alaska

A dividend for everyone

since 1982 · annual

Every resident receives a share of the common fund once a year, without a means test. For more than forty years.

A club with room to grow

The roof is not a closed circle but an offer. The blueprint is calculated for 30 European states (EU, Norway, Iceland, Switzerland) with 465.6 million people – partners beyond are possible too. Choose for yourself: once it reaches the population of the United States, the new alliance plays in the same league and is better prepared for the future.

Six are enough

Germany, the United Kingdom, France, Italy, Spain and Poland together reach the size of the United States. Every further country makes the club stronger.

Beyond Europe

The rules are not tied to Europe. They fit wherever the values match: rule of law, democracy, a social market economy and local self-government. Given the current data and model, this has not yet been worked out further.
