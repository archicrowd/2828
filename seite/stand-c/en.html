The simple tax system for the 21st century.

Fair, social and innovative.

Taxes can change the world! They can steer people towards common goals simply because it pays for them to act that way. And rules are accepted when they are fair. Nobody understands why a care worker should pay more on her income than an internet platform or an investment professional on his profits. That is why the “Golden Third” spreads all taxes and levies evenly across labour, capital and automation.

The concept develops and calculates rules that can be applied internationally: following the principles of the United Nations and the Paris Agreement, fair to the young generation and aimed at broad, sustainable prosperity.

The concept:

Few rules, one principle

Every building block has already been tried somewhere in the world. The Golden Third joins them into a system that can be explained on a single page.

Income

Graduated rates with a hard ceiling: the Golden Third

0%up to €10,720
11%up to €16,080
19%up to €21,440
25%up to €32,160
33%above — upper limit

Gross income is taxed – no deductions, no income splitting. Basic needs stay tax-free; above that, work pays at every step. The brackets lie at one, one and a half, two and three times basic needs and grow with them.

Work

No more social contributions

0% on wages

Work is no longer taxed twice. Employers pass at least 80% of the employer contributions they save into wages. These are the contributions that disappear – for health, long-term care, pensions, unemployment and family; accident insurance and levies for training, housing and transport stay with the employer. A law requires all employment contracts to be adjusted accordingly by the date of introduction. Independent monitoring of gross wages by sector accompanies the first years; any employer who fails to adjust pays the difference into the legacy fund. Denmark already levies hardly any social contributions today.

Capital

Capital and work treated alike

33% on all returns

Capital gains count too. Turning wages into capital income no longer pays.

Inheritance

Inheritance is income too

33% income tax on inheritances above €100,000

No exemptions. Whoever inherits a business or a house can defer the tax – nobody has to sell to pay it.

Land · municipalities and regions

Municipalities and regions tax the land

0.1–1% of land value

Only the value of the plot counts, not the building. Whoever builds or renovates no longer pays for it. It may not be passed on to tenants.

Wealth

Large fortunes contribute

1.5% above €2 million

Wealth up to €2 million is untouched. Above it, a minimum tax applies that can be credited against income tax.

Corporations and platforms

Taxed where the customers are

33% where the customers are

This explicitly includes digital platforms. Profits can be shifted, customers cannot.

Consumption

VAT becomes a label

A+ · 5 %basic needs, repairs
A · 11 %sustainable goods
B · 17 %standard goods
C · 25 %resource-intensive, luxury
S · 33 %harmful: tobacco, alcohol, fuels, combustion cars

As with the energy label, you see at a glance what a product costs and why. Goods are rated by their resource and carbon footprint, not by origin. 33% applies only to harmful goods. Suppliers reach a lower rate through certification by approved, state-supervised bodies – a competition for sustainability.

Climate

A carbon price returned to everyone

Common price path under the Paris Agreement · returned through the transformation dividend

Those who consume little gain. Switzerland has been returning its CO₂ levy to everyone in equal shares for years.

Dividend

Transformation dividend: every month, for everyone

Adults in full, children 80%, without applying. It gives everyone a share of what the transformation brings in: steering taxes and the gains of digitalisation.

The Strong Net

Nobody falls through

basic cover without premiums

Knotted from many knots – everyone holds, nobody falls through: health, care, a minimum pension, basic needs in case of disability and unemployment. Anyone who earns little is topped up to basic needs; every euro you earn yourself counts only by half. Beyond that is your own route with your own provision (10% of wages into your own pension account, locked until retirement age; a few investment options and a default fund for anyone who does not choose). Basic livelihood (Germany): €1,029 a month for a single person, €2,675 for a couple with two children; the net as a whole costs 9.6% of GDP (2028, today's purchasing power).

Strong municipalities and regions, strong citizens, a strong community of states

The number of administrative levels is reduced. Municipalities and regions receive their share by a fixed formula and decide more freely on local tasks and finances. The state and the community of states take on only what municipalities and regions cannot do alone – and keep at most one third of revenue for these higher-level tasks.

Why a transformation dividend?

What we produce today rests on what generations before us have built: knowledge, technology, infrastructure. More and more of it is done by machines and software. The dividend gives everyone the same share back – from what this change brings in. It is financed from the carbon price and the VAT on harmful consumption, plus the taxation of digital platforms where their customers are. The level of the dividend follows from the economic output and the tasks of each country.

It offsets what the transformation of work, energy and the economy demands of each individual. It is unbureaucratic and our common inheritance from the fruits of progress.

The individual is trusted

to manage their own money: at most one third in tax, no contributions, their own provision in their own account. A dividend every month, without applying.

Municipalities and regions are trusted

to run their own affairs: they receive fixed amounts per resident and per child, under the same rule in every country. They pay out the top-up and hardship support locally, on application; anyone whose tax and wage data suggest an entitlement receives a pre-filled application. The country sets the amounts and how income is counted. They decide on membership in districts and associations and on local support programmes.

The community of states is trusted

with what can only be done together: joint research, free movement, a market without borders, a common court and common standards. On these tasks it decides by a simple majority of member states; the ground rules themselves are changed by the states with a two-thirds majority.

Municipalities and regions do their own sums

The formula comes down to two numbers: €3,122 per inhabitant and €10,954 per child per year (average of the 30 states; every country applies the same rule to its economic output). More children therefore means more money for schools. Whatever municipalities and regions actively save on administration ends up with their citizens.

On average, 15.7% of municipal and regional budgets goes to administration. Their tasks differ from country to country, so a direct comparison of shares only goes so far.

A net that holds – and opens paths

The Golden Third combines security with freedom: nobody falls below basic needs, and whoever starts something new – a job, training, a business – does not lose their footing.

Education and care

Municipalities and regions invest in the next generation

For every child aged 5 to 19, municipalities and regions receive €10,954 each year – more children, more money. They fund schools, childcare and support locally. Children receive care, upbringing and education as services of municipalities and regions; the child dividend and the basic livelihood cover material needs.

Your own education and work

Every step pays

No social contributions on wages, at most one third tax on every euro. Anyone who earns little is topped up to basic needs; only half of your own earnings is counted. The dividend continues – during further training too, or when changing jobs.

Research and innovation

Research together, found freely

Basic research is a joint task of the community; universities are funded by the states (0.77 and 0.74 % of economic output in the overall account). Researchers and students work in a common research area. Labour, capital and automation are treated alike: whoever develops, hires or founds pays at most one third on every additional euro.

Culture and community

What matters locally is decided locally

Culture, sport and community stay with the municipalities and regions (1.22% of GDP in the overall calculation). They decide for themselves what to spend their money on – including what they save in administration.

More security and more freedom at once

The net holds without holding you back. The dividend comes without applying and does not stop when someone takes up work. The top-up to basic needs falls by only half of what you earn yourself. High rents, special needs and emergencies are handled locally by municipalities and regions – under the country's rules, in one place.

Fairer distribution? The Gini coefficient

The Gini coefficient measures the inequality of disposable incomes (0 = everyone equal, 100 = one person has everything). According to the model it falls in all five countries – dividend and basic livelihood lift the lower incomes. In France it falls only slightly: employer contributions for health and family insurance have no ceiling there, so passing them into wages also lifts high wages; the uniform cap (pass-through up to six times basic needs) limits this effect.

The transformation dividend over time

Above all, the dividend returns what was previously paid in taxes on harmful products and behaviour. As harmful emissions disappear, this part shrinks; health and care cost more as the population ages. Growth works the other way. On average across the 30 states the dividend falls from €254 (2028) to €144 (2060). More work and automation raise it again: in the middle path (more work, medium AI) it is €172 in 2060.

Six ground rules for taking part

Simple and the same for every state. What can only be done together is carried by the community of states.

Who takes part

Every state that is a member of the United Nations, has ratified both human rights covenants of 1966 and the Paris Climate Agreement and acts under the rule of law (Rule of Law Index of 0.66 or more) can take part. A state that has not yet reached the threshold can apply the common rules as a “partner”.

The same rules in every country

Every state measures everything against its own basic needs: tariff, label, dividend, strong net and the fixed amounts for municipalities and regions. State and community together keep at most one third of revenue. The fixed formula replaces endless negotiations. Nobody has to ask, nobody can cut.

What can only be done together

The community of states guarantees free movement for all citizens, a market without borders for goods, services and capital, a common research area and a common court that every individual can also turn to. Tax rules and the consumption label apply equally everywhere; insurance years count in every state. For research, standards and the court, every state pays the same share of its economic output into a common fund. On these tasks the community decides by simple majority; the ground rules themselves are changed by the states with a two-thirds majority.

Legacy burdens: half own, half shared

Every state carries half of its old debts and pension promises itself; the other half is carried by all participants, so that together they are strong. Everything is repaid by 2098. Partners carry their legacy burden themselves until they fully meet the common standards. Over-indebted states have an orderly restart procedure. Existing pensions continue. Old promises without own funding (e.g. statutory pensions, civil-service pensions) remain uncut up to twice basic needs after 35 insurance years (pro rata for shorter careers, never below basic needs); above that they are reduced in steps from the start date. Own savings are never reduced, and pensioners' health and long-term care contributions disappear. No pensioner household falls below today's minimum income in old age.

Report on sustainability

Every state reports regularly to the United Nations on the 17 Sustainable Development Goals.

Open and voluntary

Whoever meets the first rule can join; whoever leaves takes their legacy burden with them. All values are published in a public register and reassessed every two years.

What already works elsewhere

Many building blocks already exist somewhere today – often for decades.

Estonia

One rate, filed online in minutes

tax return online in minutes · land tax since 1993

The tax return is done online in a few minutes. Property tax is based on land value alone; buildings are not taxed.

Denmark

Strong municipalities, a welfare state funded by taxes

98 municipalities since 2007

Municipalities run schools, care and social services. Social contributions play hardly any role – the welfare state is paid for by taxes.

Switzerland

A CO₂ levy returned to everyone

since 2008 · the same amount for everyone

Revenue from the CO₂ levy is returned to all residents in equal shares, credited against health insurance premiums. Municipalities set their own taxes.

Alaska

A dividend for everyone

since 1982 · annual

Every resident receives a share of the common fund once a year, without a means test. For more than forty years.

Australia

Mandatory saving into your own account

since 1992 · 12% of wages since 2025

Employers must pay into each employee's own retirement account (superannuation); the money belongs to the account, not the state. Since July 2025 it is 12% of wages.

Sweden

Part of the pension in your own account

since 2000 · 2.5 of 18.5%

Of the 18.5% pension contribution, 2.5 points go into the premium pension in an individual account. Everyone chooses their own funds; those who do not choose are in the state default fund.

An open community of states on the foundation of the United Nations

The community of states is not a closed circle but an offer. Every state that meets the first ground rule can take part – wherever it lies on the map. So far the blueprint has been calculated for 30 European states (EU, Norway, Iceland, Switzerland) with 465.6 million people. A first screening shows 34 states meeting the rule; 7 more can start as partners.

What binds the community together

Free movement

Live, work and study in any participating state. Dividend and net apply where you live; insurance years from all states count together.

A market without borders

Goods, services and capital move freely. A label, once certified, applies everywhere; platforms pay tax where their customers are.

Research together

Basic research and large facilities from the common fund; open access to universities and results.

A common court

It decides disputes on the rules and freedoms of the community – between states and on the complaint of any individual.

Common standards

Label, register and data exchange between tax administrations, so that every rule applies equally everywhere.

Security as an offer

Those who wish can procure and operate defence and digital infrastructure together. This is not a condition for taking part.

Derived from the treaties of the United Nations

Every rule rests on a foundation that almost all states of the world have signed. Two rules are the project's own developments; they are at its centre.

Capital and labour taxed equally

own development; follows the principle of equality (ICCPR Art. 26): same income, same tax, wherever it comes from

VAT as a label

own development; steers consumption in line with Goal 12 and the Paris Agreement (Art. 2(1)(c): finance flows consistent with a low-emission pathway)

Method

All figures come from an open calculation that anyone can rerun, based on official statistics (Eurostat, OECD, national statistical offices and authorities) and published surveys; for Germany a synthetic microsimulation checks the distribution across all households; the countries' tax and contribution models are checked against the OECD's comparative calculation (Taxing Wages 2026), and deviations are explained in the log. Every change and every error found is logged with a number. In October 2026 the website and the calculation were recomputed in three internal reviews, carried out with a different AI model from the one used to build the calculation (including their own microsimulation); findings and responses are documented. Independent reviews are welcome, and the material is ready. Developed with the method of architectural design, using AI tools and AI-assisted calculation models.
