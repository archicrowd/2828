Today's system:

Too complex to understand. Too full of holes to be fair.

Today's system has grown layer by layer. It burdens work more than anything else and leaves citizens and municipalities frustrated. Reference country: Germany.

The concept:

Few rules, one principle

Every building block has already been tried somewhere in the world. TAX 28:28 joins them into a system that fits on a single page.

Income

Graduated rates with a firm ceiling

0%up to €12,847
9%up to €19,270
16%up to €25,694
21%up to €38,541
28%above — upper limit

Gross income is taxed – no deductions, no income splitting. The subsistence minimum stays tax-free; above it, work pays at every step. The brackets start at one, one and a half, two and three times the basic need where you live and rise with it.

Work

No more social contributions

0% on wages

Work is no longer taxed twice. Countries that fund their welfare state from taxes do it this way – Denmark levies hardly any social contributions.

Capital

Capital and work treated alike

28% on all returns

Capital gains count too. Turning wages into capital income no longer pays.

Inheritance

Inherited wealth is income too

28% above €100,000 per heir

No exemptions. Whoever inherits a business or a house can defer the tax – nobody has to sell to pay it.

Land · municipality

The municipality taxes the land

0.1–1% of land value

Only the value of the plot counts, not the building. Whoever builds or renovates no longer pays for it. It may not be passed on to tenants.

Wealth

Large fortunes contribute

1.5% above €2 million

Wealth up to €2 million is untouched. Above it, a minimum tax applies that can be credited against income tax.

Corporations and platforms

Taxed where the customers are

28% at the place of use

This explicitly includes digital platforms. Profits can be shifted, customers cannot.

Consumption

VAT becomes a label

A+ · 5 %basic needs, repairs
A · 11 %sustainable goods
B · 17 %standard goods
C · 22 %resource-intensive
D · 28 %climate-damaging luxury

Like an energy label, you see at a glance what a product costs and why. Products are rated by their resource and CO₂ footprint, not by their origin.

Climate

A carbon price returned to everyone

European price path · returned via the dividend

Those who consume little gain. Switzerland has been returning its CO₂ levy to everyone in equal shares for years.

Dividend

Every month, for everyone

Adults in full, children half, no application. Every country pays out the same share of its economic output. It is what remains after the tasks are paid – and it grows with the economy.

Protection

Nobody falls behind

guarantee of current benefits · hardship fund

Whoever receives basic income support or student aid today (in Germany: Bürgergeld, Grundsicherung, BAföG) keeps at least the current level. The fund also covers housing costs, care and special circumstances.

Strong municipalities, strong citizens, a strong European roof

Instead of five levels there are two. The municipality receives most of the money and decides for itself on its finances and on what it passes on to other administrations. A European roof takes on only what no municipality can do alone – and may not take more than the treaty allows.

Why a dividend?

What we produce today rests on what generations before us built: knowledge, technology, infrastructure. More and more of it is done by machines and software. The dividend gives everyone an equal share of this jointly inherited progress – a return from the gains of automation. Every country pays out the same share of its own economic output: equal rights for all, starting from one's own country. On average across the club it covers at least half the subsistence minimum; where output is lower today, it starts below that and grows under the same rules.

And it offsets what the transformation of work, energy and the economy asks of each of us: whoever has to change course does not stand empty-handed.

The individual is trusted

to manage their own money: one rate instead of a tax jungle, no special rules. A dividend every month, without application.

The municipality is trusted

to run its own affairs: it receives fixed amounts per resident and per child, under the same rule in every country. It decides on membership in districts and associations and on local support programmes.

The roof is trusted

with what can only be done together: defence and digital sovereignty, a single market without borders, common standards. Nothing more. The roof decides by majority of its members.

The dividend grows with the economy

Until 2032, old mistakes have to be mended: defence spending has been raised, and old debts must keep being serviced at higher interest. After that the dividend grows with the economy – and the debt melts away by itself: from 81% to 32% of economic output, without a single euro of repayment.

Why the roof cannot grow out of bounds

A common roof is only as good as its limits. Five rules are written into the treaty.

A cap, not an allocation

The roof keeps at most 32% of what it collects – and only what its tasks really cost.

What is not needed flows through

Every euro above need goes to the municipalities and into the dividend. A limit that need not be used up never becomes an entitlement.

Formula instead of bargaining

Municipalities receive fixed amounts per resident and per child. Nobody has to ask, nobody can cut.

Carrying old debt together

The roof takes over interest payments and guarantees a common rate. Whoever brings more old debt evens this out over thirty years – the municipalities do not notice.

Deciding by majority

An alliance in which everyone has a veto cannot decide when the moment of decision comes.

What already works elsewhere

Nothing in TAX 28:28 is a shot in the dark. Every building block is already running somewhere today – often for decades.

Estonia

One rate, filed online in minutes

22% flat rate · land tax since 1993

The tax return is done online in a few minutes. Property tax is based on land value alone; buildings are not taxed.

Denmark

Strong municipalities, a welfare state funded by taxes

98 municipalities since 2007

Municipalities run schools, care and social services. Social contributions play hardly any role – the welfare state is paid for by taxes.

Switzerland

A CO₂ levy returned to everyone

since 2008 · the same amount for everyone

Revenue from the CO₂ levy is returned to all residents in equal shares, credited against health insurance premiums. Municipalities set their own taxes.

Alaska

A dividend for everyone

since 1982 · annual

Every resident receives a share of the common fund once a year, without a means test. For more than forty years.

A club with room to grow

The roof is not a closed circle but an offer. It has been calculated for ten countries – more than thirty in Europe are possible, and partners beyond. Choose for yourself: once it reaches the population of the United States, the club plays in the same league.

Six are enough

Germany, the United Kingdom, France, Italy, Spain and Poland together match the population of the United States. Every further country makes the club stronger; none makes it impossible.
